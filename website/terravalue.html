how it works

one address. four steps. real numbers.

TerraValue connects to live public ArcGIS parcel databases across 10 Georgia jurisdictions. It pulls your lot size, assessed value, and canopy data — then runs every number through peer-reviewed USDA and EPA methodologies.

The result: a complete picture of what your land's ecosystem is worth in actual dollars, broken down across six services, with a 0-100 stewardship score and a branded PDF report you can take to a meeting.

Cascading ArcGIS lookup: city endpoint first, county fallback. Lot size, assessed value, canopy %.

what we measure

six ecosystem services, quantified

Every value is calculated from peer-reviewed research. Every formula is traceable to a source paper. No vibes.

$663/yr

carbon sequestration

Trees absorb CO2 and store it as biomass. We calculate annual sequestration per canopy acre and price it at the EPA social cost of carbon.

2.6 t CO2/acre/yr (Atlanta iTree Eco) x $255/tonne (EPA SC-GHG 2023)

$520/yr

stormwater management

Canopy intercepts 35% of rainfall. We calculate gallons diverted from the storm system and price it at municipal avoided treatment costs.

USDA CUFR Fact Sheet #4 x $4.00/1,000 gal municipal average

$418/yr

air quality improvement

Trees filter PM2.5, ozone, NO2, and SO2 from the air. Atlanta's PM2.5 concentration makes this one of the highest-value services.

Nowak et al. 2014, BenMAP weighted x $142K/ton PM2.5 (Atlanta)

$350/yr

energy savings

Strategic canopy reduces cooling loads in summer and wind exposure in winter. We calculate avoided kWh per canopy acre.

McPherson 2003, Atlanta iTree Eco: 1,800 kWh/acre/yr

$320/yr

habitat value

Canopy provides nesting, foraging, and corridor habitat. We use willingness-to-pay studies to price the public value of urban biodiversity.

Troy & Wilson 2006: $320/acre canopy (WTP studies)

+7%

property value premium

Mature canopy coverage adds a measurable premium to assessed property values. This is often the single largest ecosystem service dollar amount.

Kovacs et al. 2022, USDA NRS meta-analysis: 7% premium
